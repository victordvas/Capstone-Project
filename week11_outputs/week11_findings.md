### What I learned from this run

I compared the methods on **2,500 loans** using the same six transformed financial features. In the original units, `annual_inc` accounted for 83.0% of squared distance across the sampled pairs. After preprocessing, the largest absolute standardized value was still 35.4. Scaling changed the units, but unusual observations did not disappear. The strongest transformed-feature correlation was -0.488 between `fico_range_low` and `revol_util`. That identifies the strongest linear relationship here; it does not rule out nonlinear relationships.

For DBSCAN, the displayed setting was **eps = 1.166, MinPts = 12**. It produced 1 clusters, with 2,000 core points, 283 border points, and 217 noise points. Coverage was 91.3%, and the assigned-point silhouette was not defined. None of the tested settings passed all the screens, so this is the reference setting I specified before looking at the results. I would not present it as evidence that DBSCAN found a useful segmentation. With fewer than two assigned clusters, a between-cluster silhouette is not defined. Across the tested settings, noise ranged from 1.2% to 32.3%. The noise category's median income was $55,000 and median DTI was 13.0. Those summaries help describe the excluded loans, but do not make them one coherent group.

For HAC, I kept **ward linkage with 2 groups**, with a silhouette of 0.204. It had the highest silhouette among the cuts that passed the screens. There were fewer than two eligible HAC cuts to compare. The score is closer to zero than to one, so I would be cautious about presenting the groups as clearly separated. The K-means reference at the same group count had a silhouette of 0.208. Both use the same evaluation pool, while the original DBSCAN silhouette excludes noise. These scores tell me about separation under the chosen distance; they are not repayment accuracy.

The largest HAC group was Cluster 2, with 1,632 loans. Its median loan amount was $16,000, annual income was $77,946, DTI was 18.5, and FICO was 685. Those values give me a clearer description than a cluster number alone. The two-component PCA plot retains 58.0% of feature variation, so I would not judge separation from that picture alone.

When I refit on 80% subsets, HAC ARI ranged from 0.304 to 0.497. DBSCAN did not produce at least two assigned groups in both partitions for any of the subsample ARI checks, so those ARIs are not defined. DBSCAN noise-status agreement ranged from 96.2% to 98.8%. These are three overlapping-sample checks, not proof that the groups will hold up on new borrowers.

After fixing the groups, the highest observed HAC charge-off share was 20.3% in Cluster 2 (1,632 loans). The lowest was 18.5% in Cluster 1 (868 loans), a difference of 1.8 percentage points. I would read that difference alongside the group sizes and intervals. It is an association in this sample, and it does not show that the group characteristics caused the outcome.

For my capstone, the value is in understanding which profiles group together, which loans are left out, and how much the answer changes with the method. I would check whether these patterns appear in later-issued loans before treating them as useful for ongoing loan review.
